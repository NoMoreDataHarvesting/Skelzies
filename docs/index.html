Skelzies for iPad

Privacy Policy

Effective August 1, 2026 · Last updated August 1, 2026

Skelzies is an offline iPad game that recreates the classic New York City street game of skelly. It collects nothing. No accounts, no network, no analytics, no ads, no data of any kind leaving your device.

1.Information We Collect

None. Skelzies does not collect, store, transmit, sell, or share any personal information or usage data. Specifically, the App does not:

  • Require an account, sign-in, email address, or any registration
  • Collect your name, address, phone number, or contact information
  • Access your contacts, photos, camera, microphone, calendar, or health data
  • Request or use your location
  • Collect device identifiers, advertising identifiers (IDFA), or fingerprinting data
  • Gather analytics, crash reports, telemetry, or usage statistics
  • Display advertising or offer in-app purchases
  • Use cookies, web beacons, or similar tracking technologies

2.Network Access

Skelzies contains no networking code. The App makes no network requests, connects to no servers, and requires no internet connection. It works identically in Airplane Mode. There is no backend service associated with the App, because none is needed.

3.Information Stored on Your Device

At the start of a match you may type nicknames for the 2–6 players. Those nicknames — along with scores, turn order, and game progress — exist only in the device's temporary memory while the game is running. They are not written to disk, saved to a database, backed up, or synced to iCloud. When the match ends or you close the App, that information is gone.

The App creates no files, keeps no history, and stores no preferences between launches.

4.Third-Party Services and SDKs

Skelzies includes no third-party software development kits, frameworks, libraries, analytics providers, advertising networks, or social media integrations. Every graphic in the game is drawn in code by the App itself. The only software components used are Apple's own operating system frameworks (SwiftUI, SpriteKit, UIKit), which run locally on your device.

5.Tracking

Skelzies does not track you. The App does not link any data to your identity and does not follow you across other companies' apps or websites. Because no tracking occurs, the App does not present Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt.

6.Apple's Role

Skelzies is distributed through the Apple App Store. Apple may independently collect certain information related to your download and use of the App — such as purchase records, or crash diagnostics if you have opted into sharing them with developers. That data is handled by Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy, not by us, and we receive only anonymized, aggregate reporting from Apple that cannot identify any individual user.

7.Children's Privacy

Skelzies is a family-friendly game suitable for all ages. Because the App collects no personal information whatsoever, it collects none from children. We comply with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) by design: there is nothing to collect, no way to contact other users, no user-generated content shared outside the device, and no advertising.

8.Data Security

Since no data leaves your device, there is no transmission to intercept and no server-side database that could be breached. Anything you type during a match is protected by your device's own security and disappears when the App closes.

9.Your Rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR (EU/UK), the CCPA/CPRA (California), and similar regulations grant rights to access, correct, delete, or port your personal data, and to opt out of its sale or sharing. We have no personal data about you to access, correct, delete, port, sell, or share — so these rights are satisfied by default. Deleting the App removes it and everything associated with it from your device. We do not sell or share personal information, and never have.

10.Changes to This Policy

If the App ever changes in a way that affects privacy, this policy will be updated and the "Last updated" date above will change. Material changes will also be noted in the App Store release notes for the version that introduces them. Prior versions remain visible in this page's commit history.
